    Text as a sequence

    • Bag of words counts words but loses order: "anjing gigit orang" and "orang gigit anjing" look the same.
    • N-grams keep a little order, but the table becomes huge and mostly empty.
    • Pipeline: text → tokens → index numbers → embedding vectors.
    • Padding makes sequences the same length; masking tells the model to ignore the padding.

    Recurrent neural networks

    • An RNN reads one step at a time and keeps a hidden state hₜ, its memory.
    • hₜ = tanh(Wₓxₜ + Wₕhₜ₋₁ + b): what I see now plus what I remember.
    • The same weights are reused at every step, so any length works.
    • Training multiplies by Wₕ at every step backwards: gradients vanish (or explode), so early words are forgotten.

    LSTM, GRU and bidirectional RNNs

    • LSTM adds a cell state, a conveyor belt of memory, controlled by 3 gates: forget, input, output.
    • cₜ = f × cₜ₋₁ + i × c̃ₜ. If f = 1, memory is kept, so gradients survive much longer.
    • GRU has 2 gates (update, reset), no separate cell state, about 25% fewer weights. Accuracy is usually similar.
    • Bidirectional: read forwards and backwards (not for real-time). Stacked: return_sequences=True on every layer except the last.

    In the explorer: switch to LSTM. Its input gate ignores neutral words like "the" and "was", so the key word is not washed out.

    Speech as a sequence

    • A waveform has 16,000 samples per second: too many to use directly.
    • Cut it into 25 ms frames every 10 ms (about 100 frames per second), then compute a mel spectrogram or MFCC per frame.
    • Model: CNN + BiLSTM, or a Transformer. Classification is many-to-one, like sentiment.
    • Speech-to-text uses CTC loss to align frames with letters. Today: fine-tune wav2vec 2.0 or Whisper.

    Attention and self-attention

    • Encoder–decoder RNNs squeeze a whole sentence into one vector: long sentences lose detail.
    • Attention lets each output word look back at all input words and choose where to focus.
    • Self-attention: words in one sentence look at each other. Query = what I look for, Key = what I offer, Value = what I pass on.
    • Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ) V gives context-aware word vectors.

    Transformers and pretrained models

    • Transformer block: multi-head self-attention, feed-forward, residual connections and layer norm, repeated N times. Positional encoding adds word order.
    • Encoder only (BERT) for understanding; decoder only (GPT) for generating; encoder–decoder (T5, Whisper) for translation and speech-to-text.
    • Pretrain once on huge unlabelled data, then fine-tune with a small learning rate on your task.
    • Transformers are parallel and handle long-range context, but attention cost grows with n².

    Challenges of supervised deep learning

    • Data: deep models need many labels; small data often favours simple models or pretrained ones. Watch class imbalance.
    • Generalisation: overfitting, domain shift (movie reviews → Manglish tweets), and fragile, overconfident predictions.
    • Compute: GPUs, latency, model size and energy. Report them, not only accuracy.
    • Trust: black-box decisions, bias learned from data, and privacy (PDPA).
