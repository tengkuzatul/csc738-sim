    Neural networks and training Week 6

    • A neuron computes a weighted sum plus a bias, then an activation (ReLU in hidden layers, softmax at the output).
    • Learning loop: forward pass → loss → backpropagation → weight update.
    • Optimisers: SGD, SGD with momentum, Adam. The learning rate is the most important setting.
    • Regularisation: dropout, data augmentation, early stopping, weight decay.

    In the explorer: set the learning rate to 3 and press Train. The loss jumps around instead of falling smoothly. Then try 0.01: it barely moves.

    Convolutional Neural Networks Week 6

    • A filter (kernel) slides across the image: multiply and add gives one value of the feature map.
    • The same filter is used everywhere (weight sharing), so far fewer weights than a dense network.
    • ReLU keeps positive responses; max pooling halves the size and keeps the strongest signal.
    • Flatten → dense layers → softmax gives the class probabilities.

    Transfer learning and fine-tuning Week 6

    • Start from a network pre-trained on ImageNet instead of random weights.
    • 1. Load the base, 2. freeze it, 3. add and train a new head.
    • 4. Fine-tune: unfreeze the top layers and train with a very small learning rate (e.g. 0.00001).
    • Works well with a few hundred images per class.

    Heavy-weight vs light-weight CNNs Week 7

    • Heavy (VGG16, ResNet50, InceptionV3): more parameters, usually for servers with GPUs.
    • Light (MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, SqueezeNet): designed for phones and edge devices.
    • Variations: bilinear, multi-scale, multi-view and multi-region CNNs.
    • Always compare accuracy together with size and speed.

    Object detection and recognition Week 7

    • Classification gives one label; detection gives many boxes, each with a label and a confidence.
    • Two-stage (R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, Faster R-CNN): propose regions, then classify. More accurate, slower.
    • One-stage (YOLO, SSD, RetinaNet): boxes and classes in one pass. Fast enough for video.
    • Segmentation: FCN and U-Net (every pixel), Mask R-CNN (a mask per object).

    Try it: non-maximum suppression (NMS)

    A detector predicted five boxes around two leaves. NMS keeps the most confident box, removes any box that overlaps it by more than the IoU threshold, and repeats.

    6

    Evaluating deep learning models Week 7

    • Classification: confusion matrix, per-class recall, macro F1, training curves, Grad-CAM.
    • Detection: IoU decides whether a box is correct; precision, recall and mAP summarise it.
    • mAP@0.5 uses IoU ≥ 0.5; mAP@0.5:0.95 averages stricter thresholds too.
    • Also report model size, inference time and robustness.
